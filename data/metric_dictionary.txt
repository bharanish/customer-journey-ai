Metrics Dictionary

Conversion Rate

Definition:
Conversion Rate is the percentage of users who complete a desired action compared to the total number of users who had the opportunity to perform that action.

Formula:
Conversion Rate = (Conversions / Total Visitors) × 100

Business Importance:
Conversion Rate is one of the most important performance indicators in digital marketing and e-commerce. A higher conversion rate typically indicates that marketing campaigns, website experience, and product offerings are effectively meeting customer needs.

Example:
If 1,000 visitors arrive on a website and 50 complete a purchase, the conversion rate is 5%.

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Definition:
Customer Acquisition Cost measures the average cost required to acquire a new customer.

Formula:
CAC = Total Sales and Marketing Cost / Number of New Customers Acquired

Business Importance:
CAC helps organizations evaluate the efficiency of marketing and sales investments. Businesses should strive to keep CAC lower than Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

Example:
If a company spends $10,000 on marketing and acquires 100 customers, CAC equals $100.

---

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

Definition:
Customer Lifetime Value represents the total revenue expected from a customer throughout the entire relationship with the business.

Formula:
CLV = Average Revenue Per Customer × Average Customer Lifespan

Business Importance:
CLV helps determine customer profitability and supports investment decisions in acquisition and retention strategies.

---

Average Order Value (AOV)

Definition:
Average Order Value measures the average revenue generated per customer transaction.

Formula:
AOV = Total Revenue / Number of Orders

Business Importance:
Increasing AOV can significantly improve profitability without increasing customer acquisition costs.

---

Retention Rate

Definition:
Retention Rate measures the percentage of customers who continue to engage with a business over a specified period.

Formula:
Retention Rate = ((Customers at End of Period - New Customers) / Customers at Start of Period) × 100

Business Importance:
Retention is often more cost-effective than acquisition and is strongly correlated with profitability.

---

Churn Rate

Definition:
Churn Rate measures the percentage of customers who stop using a product or service during a specified period.

Formula:
Churn Rate = Lost Customers / Total Customers at Beginning of Period

Business Importance:
A high churn rate may indicate customer dissatisfaction, competitive pressure, or poor product-market fit.

---

Net Promoter Score (NPS)

Definition:
NPS measures customer loyalty and willingness to recommend a company to others.

Formula:
NPS = % Promoters - % Detractors

Score Categories:
Promoters: 9-10
Passives: 7-8
Detractors: 0-6

Business Importance:
NPS is commonly used to assess customer satisfaction and predict future growth.

---

Revenue Per User (RPU)

Definition:
Revenue Per User measures average revenue generated per customer.

Formula:
RPU = Total Revenue / Total Customers

Business Importance:
RPU helps evaluate monetization effectiveness and customer value.

---

Return on Investment (ROI)

Definition:
ROI measures profitability relative to investment cost.

Formula:
ROI = ((Gain from Investment - Cost of Investment) / Cost of Investment) × 100

Business Importance:
ROI is used to evaluate marketing campaigns, technology investments, and strategic initiatives.

---

Monthly Active Users (MAU)

Definition:
MAU represents the number of unique users who interact with a product during a calendar month.

Business Importance:
MAU is a key growth metric for digital products and subscription businesses.

---

Daily Active Users (DAU)

Definition:
DAU represents the number of unique users who interact with a product during a single day.

Business Importance:
DAU measures engagement and product stickiness.

---

DAU/MAU Ratio

Definition:
DAU/MAU Ratio measures user engagement frequency.

Formula:
DAU / MAU × 100

Business Importance:
A higher ratio indicates stronger customer engagement and habitual usage patterns.
